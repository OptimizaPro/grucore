What this means for Z Construction:
- **About 40% of Lee County homes (and 46% of Auburn's) were built 2000–2019.** Their original builder-grade kitchens, baths, carpet and cabinets are 7–25 years old, which is the classic window for a first refresh. That is exactly Z's mix: cabinet painting and refinishing, LVP flooring, bath conversions, interior paint and kitchen updates. Stone Martin Builders and other production-built subdivisions make up a large share of this stock.
- **Opelika has the older stock** (48% built before 1990, including 12.5% from the 1950s–40s). That means more full-gut baths, repairs, rot and subfloor work, and additions onto small 1960s–70s ranches.
- **Valley/Lanett, Tuskegee, Phenix City and Columbus** have the oldest, lowest-value stock. Those are repair-heavy and price-sensitive markets.

### A9. Licensing and permits

**Alabama Home Builders Licensure Board (HBLB)**, which covers residential work [verified]:
- A license is required for anyone doing "construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, or reimprovement of a residential structure where the **cost of the undertaking including labor and materials exceeds $10,000**." This covers single-family homes, duplexes, triplexes and fourplexes. https://hblb.alabama.gov/faqs-applicants/ and https://hblb.alabama.gov/law/
- There are three license types:
  - **Unlimited**, which requires the Alabama Home Builders Exam and covers building plus roofing.
  - **Limited**, which needs a separate roofing license for roofing work.
  - **Roofers**.
- Reciprocity (skills section waived) applies to TN, SC, MS, LA and NC. The processing fee is $100. The HBLB law itself does not require insurance, but other law and contracts may.
- Residential roofing reportedly triggers licensing at **$2,500** [secondary; verify at hblb.alabama.gov/exemptions/].
- **Marketing implication.** If Z holds an HBLB license, display the **license number** on every page footer, in the GBP description and on quotes; it is a strong trust signal. If it does not, jobs must stay at or under $10,000 per undertaking, and the site should not advertise additions or full remodels. Don't split contracts to dodge the threshold.

**Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors** [verified]:
- The threshold rose from **$50,000 to $100,000** under Act 2024-277, effective **Oct 1, 2024**. It applies to general contractors and subcontractors on non-residential work; residential work falls under the HBLB. The $50k figure in older guides is outdated. https://genconbd.alabama.gov/14104/changes-to-the-alabama-licensing-board-for-general-contractors-section-of-the-code-of-alabama/

**Georgia** (if serving Columbus or LaGrange):

### B3. How Spanish speakers in the US actually search and contact contractors

1. **Mixed-language, device-language driven.** About two-thirds of digital US Hispanics have searched in Spanish, but only 16% use Spanish "most or all of the time" online, versus 28% who are Spanish-dominant at home. Bilingual users write shorter, English-leaning queries; Spanish-dominant users write broader Spanish queries [secondary, ThinkNow/Google]. https://thinknow.com/blog/3-things-to-keep-in-mind-when-engaging-u-s-hispanics-via-google-search/
   - So the **English pages must also carry Spanish trust signals**: "Hablamos español" in the header, a Spanish CTA and a language toggle. Many Hispanic homeowners will land on English pages from English queries.
2. **"Cerca de mí" plus the map pack.** Spanish-language local searches lean on "cerca de mí" and Maps rather than city names; no Spanish city-modified queries autocompleted in Lee County. Winning the **map pack** through GBP proximity, reviews and categories matters more than Spanish city pages.
3. **Spanglish trade terms.** handyman, roofing, drywall, "remodelación de kitchen" and "LVP" are used as-is. Include them naturally in /es copy, e.g., "instalación de pisos LVP (vinílico de lujo)" and "reparación de drywall (tablaroca / sheetrock)". Regional variants to seed:
   - gabinetes / alacenas
   - azulejo / baldosa / cerámica
   - regadera (MX) / ducha
   - tablaroca (MX) / pladur
   - techo / roofing
4. **Mobile, WhatsApp, Facebook**
   - Pew (Feb–Jun 2025 survey): Hispanic adults use Instagram at 62% versus 50% of all adults, and are more likely than White adults to use WhatsApp and TikTok [verified]. Secondary reports put Hispanic WhatsApp use at about 54–56%, versus 32% of all adults [secondary]. https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/
   - **Add a WhatsApp click-to-chat button** (wa.me link) on /es, and **enable WhatsApp chat on GBP**: Edit profile → Contact → Chat → WhatsApp. It is available in select regions, and if both WhatsApp and SMS are enabled, only SMS shows [verified, Google help]. https://support.google.com/business/answer/15013580
   - Facebook groups ("Latinos unidos Opelika y Auburn"), Facebook Marketplace "services" posts, and church and word-of-mouth referrals drive Hispanic demand. Post before/after reels in Spanish, and answer Marketplace and Messenger inquiries in Spanish.
5. **Spanish reviews.** Ask satisfied Spanish-speaking clients to review **in Spanish** on Google and Facebook. Google shows reviews in their original language, with translation, to users. Reply in the reviewer's language. Spanish reviews containing words like "remodelación de baño", "pintura" or "Auburn" add relevance and signal to Spanish searchers that the business really serves them.

### B4. Bilingual technical SEO (Next.js, / and /es)

- **hreflang** [verified, Google doc: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions]
  - Each EN page gets `hreflang="en-US"` (self), `hreflang="es-US"` (the /es equivalent) and `hreflang="x-default"` (the EN URL). Each /es page carries the same three.
  - Use absolute URLs, keep annotations reciprocal, and let each page self-reference.
  - You can emit these from Next.js `generateMetadata` using `alternates: { canonical, languages: { 'en-US': ..., 'es-US': ..., 'x-default': ... } }`.
  - Also set `<html lang="es-US">` (or `es`) on /es routes.
- **Canonicals.** Each language page is canonical to itself; never point /es canonicals at the EN page.
- **Translate, don't mirror.** Localize the /es pages: Spanish URL slugs (/es/servicios/remodelacion-de-banos), translated titles and meta descriptions, Spanish alt text, Spanish schema `description` values, and Spanish FAQs that reflect how Hispanic clients actually ask questions (financing, deposits, "¿tienen licencia y seguro?", "¿pueden trabajar con mi seguro?").
- **Don't auto-redirect by IP or Accept-Language.** Offer a visible toggle instead. Google crawls mostly from US IPs with English headers.
- **Sitemap.** Include both language URLs; a sitemap can carry hreflang via `xhtml:link`.
- **Page speed.** Many Hispanic users are mobile-first. Keep Core Web Vitals green, and compress project photos (next/image, AVIF/WebP).

### B5. Google Business Profile (GBP) playbook

**One profile only.** Google allows one GBP per business, and duplicate listings by language risk suspension. Run one profile, with bilingual "entry points" through the website, posts and review replies [secondary, consensus of GBP product experts]. https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177

**Business name.** Use the exact legal/brand name: "Z Construction & Remodeling". Don't add "Auburn AL Kitchen Remodel" or "Se Habla Español". Whitespark ranks keywords in the business title as the #3 pack factor, but stuffing violates guidelines and invites suspension; only a real DBA/brand change is safe.

**Categories.** Names are from a third-party list of the GBP taxonomy; confirm in the GBP dashboard [secondary].
- Primary category is the #1 local pack factor in Whitespark's 2026 report. Choose the one that matches the biggest revenue line:
  - **Primary: "Remodeler"** (broadest fit for remodeling, additions, kitchen/bath). A kitchen/bath-heavy alternative is "Kitchen remodeler" or "Bathroom remodeler". Test for 60–90 days before switching.
- Secondary categories (up to 9; keep them relevant, ordered by revenue):
  1. Kitchen remodeler
  2. Bathroom remodeler
  3. General contractor
  4. Painter
  5. Flooring contractor
  6. Handyman/Handywoman/Handyperson (only if Z takes small repair jobs)
  7. Home builder or Construction company (only if Z does additions/new builds)
  8. Dry wall contractor / Tile contractor / Deck builder (pick only what is truly offered)
  9. "Cabinet refacing" does not appear to be a category; cover cabinet painting and refinishing under Services.

**Services.** Add every service as a predefined plus a custom service with descriptions. Whitespark 2026 moved predefined services from factor #81 to #22 [verified, Whitespark]. Suggested list:
- Kitchen remodeling; bathroom remodeling; tub-to-shower conversion; home additions; interior painting; exterior painting; cabinet painting & refinishing
- LVP/laminate/tile flooring; drywall repair; trim & carpentry; rental turnover / make-ready; deck & porch repair
- Spanish-speaking estimates ("Estimados en español").

**Language.** Google's "language assistance" attributes launched for healthcare (Dec 2021) and expanded to HVAC (May 2023). The languages cited were ASL, Arabic, Cantonese, English, Filipino, French and German; **Spanish was not listed**, and I could not confirm availability for remodelers [UNVERIFIED]. https://www.seroundtable.com/google-business-profiles-expanding-language-assistance-options-35399.html
- Check Edit profile → More → attributes in the dashboard. If "Spanish" or "Language assistance" appears, enable it.
- Either way, write the **description primarily in English with one clear Spanish sentence**, e.g.: "Family-owned, licensed & insured remodeling contractor in Auburn, AL... **Hablamos español — estimados gratis en español.**" Mention "Spanish-speaking" in services and posts.
- Community experts recommend one primary language to avoid a spammy mix, so keep the Spanish to one or two sentences.

**Service area / address**
- If Z works from home, **hide the address**. Add up to **20 service areas**, within about 2 hours' drive [verified, Google guidelines].
- Suggested list: Auburn, Opelika, Smiths Station, Phenix City, Beauregard, Loachapoka, Waverly, Notasulga, Salem, Tuskegee, Valley, Lanett, Dadeville/Lake Martin, Lee County AL, Russell County AL, Macon County AL, plus Columbus GA and LaGrange GA only if GA-licensed.

**2025–26 GBP changes to act on**
- **Q&A is gone.** The Q&A API was discontinued Nov 3, 2025 and public Q&A is being phased out. It has been replaced by Gemini **"Ask Maps"** (public March 12, 2026), which answers questions from the profile data, reviews, photos and **website** [secondary]. https://www.impactmarketing.net/blog/google-business-profile-qa/
  - Put FAQ answers on the website and in the GBP description, services and posts, so Ask Maps and AI can cite them.
- **WhatsApp/SMS chat** has replaced Google's native chat [verified, Google help].
- **Review policy tightened (April 2026).** Staff review quotas, employee-name solicitation, kiosks and pre-screening ("review gating") are banned. Review replies may enter moderation [secondary, Slashpost/Launchcodex]. Ask **every** client, with the same neutral wording.
- **Freshness.** Profiles quiet for 30+ days reportedly lose visibility, and photos now sort by recency [secondary].

**Cadence (recommended)**
- **Posts:** 1–2 per week (project spotlight, seasonal tip, offer). Alternate English and Spanish posts, or do a short bilingual caption.
- **Photos:** 5–10 new job photos per week during active projects (before, during and after, geotag not required), plus team photos; also short videos.
- **Reviews:** aim for a **steady 3–6 new reviews per month**, every month.
  - Whitespark 2026 says recency and steady flow beat bursts.
  - BrightLocal LCRS 2026: 74% of consumers look for reviews from the last 3 months; 31% only use businesses rated 4.5+; 47% won't consider a business with fewer than 20 reviews [secondary summaries of BrightLocal]. https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/

  - Ask for reviews that mention the **service and the town**, but don't script them.
- **Products:** consider "Cabinet Refresh Package", "Tub-to-Shower Conversion", "Rental Turnover Package" and "Interior Paint (per room)", each with a photo and a starting price.

### B8. Structured data (JSON-LD)

- **Site-wide (home/contact): `GeneralContractor`** (schema.org subtype of HomeAndConstructionBusiness → LocalBusiness). Include:
  - `name`, `url`, `logo`, `image`, `telephone`, `email`
  - `address` (or omit street if hidden; keep locality/region/postal)
  - `geo`, `areaServed` (array of City/AdministrativeArea objects), `openingHoursSpecification`, `priceRange`
  - `knowsLanguage: ["en", "es"]`
  - `sameAs` (GBP/Maps URL, Yelp, Facebook, BBB, Houzz, Angi, Instagram, YouTube)
  - `hasCredential` / `award` for the HBLB license
  - `founder`, `foundingDate`, `slogan`, and `hasOfferCatalog` listing services
  - On /es, set `inLanguage: "es-US"` on WebPage and use Spanish `description`.
  - Consider `@type: ["GeneralContractor","HousePainter"]` if painting is a major line.
- **`Service`** on each service page: `serviceType`, `provider` (@id ref to the business), `areaServed`, `description`, `offers` (optional price ranges).
- **`BreadcrumbList`** on all inner pages. It still produces rich results.
- **`FAQPage`.** FAQ rich results **stopped showing in Google on May 7, 2026**, and Google is removing report and tooling support in June–August 2026. The markup is harmless and still machine-readable [verified, SEJ]. Keep visible FAQs (valuable for users, Ask Maps and AI engines); the markup is optional. https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-drops-faq-rich-results-from-search/574429/
- **`HowTo`.** Rich results were deprecated in 2023. Use step-by-step headings in content for readability and AI extraction, not for rich results.
- **`Review` / `AggregateRating`.** Google won't show review stars for LocalBusiness/Organization when the business controls the reviews (self-serving), including embedded widgets. You may mark up individual testimonials as `Review` for semantics, but expect no stars, and **never fabricate or copy Google review ratings into markup**. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet
- **`ImageObject` / `CreativeWork` or `Article`** for Our Work case studies and blog posts, with `author` (the owner, a real person) and `datePublished`/`dateModified`.
- **Google's own position (May 2026 AI guide):** "Structured data isn't required for generative AI search, and there's no special schema.org markup you need to add." Schema helps entity clarity and rich results, but it isn't a magic GEO lever [verified]. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide

### B9. AI search / generative engine optimization (GEO/AEO) in 2026

**What drives each engine for local contractor recommendations**
- **Google AI Overviews / AI Mode / Ask Maps / Gemini**
  - Google says its AI features use the core Search index and ranking systems, with "no additional requirements" and no special files or markup. It lists keeping the **Business Profile** up to date among its business recommendations (AI optimization guide, updated July 10, 2026) [verified].
  - AI Mode increasingly cites Google's own Business Profile cards for local queries; google.com's share of AI Mode citations rose sharply in mid-2026 [secondary, BrightEdge/SE Ranking reports]. **GBP completeness plus reviews is the #1 GEO lever for Google.**
  - Gemini is grounded in live Google Maps data [secondary].
- **ChatGPT**
  - Search officially runs on a Bing-class index plus OpenAI's own crawl. There are credible reports (The Information; independent tests) that it also pulls Google results via SerpApi [secondary]. https://searchengineland.com/openai-chatgpt-serpapi-google-search-results-461226
  - **Local business cards are grounded overwhelmingly in Yelp.** A Steady Demand study of 2,880 prompts, published Aug 21, 2026, found Yelp in about 96% of structured local grounding and **Foursquare at 0%**.
